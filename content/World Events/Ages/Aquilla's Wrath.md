## The Rising Tide
For more than three thousand years, the seas rose. They rose first in a torrent, when [[The Sinking of the Cauldron]] set loose a flood that climbed several feet a year and sent tidal waves crashing across the world. For a time the rising slowed, as the sea poured away into [[The Drowning of Vhaldrun|Vhaldrun]], until [[The Sealing]] brought the torrent roaring back. And after the [[Arcanus Collapse]] it slowed again, to a creep so gentle that a harbormaster would need a decade or more of tide marks to be sure of it. Yet it never stopped. Coastlines vanished, islands shrank to nothing, valleys became bays, and whole nations sank beneath the waves. The mountains themselves were drowned, and with them went the lairs and hoards of the dragons who would not leave them.

At the heart of it all lay the cauldron of Magnus the Magnificent, sunk deep beneath the sea and pouring endlessly into it.

## The Silence of the Gods
For five hundred years, the god who could most surely have stopped the flood was [[Arkaius]] himself, whose domain touched all things. He did not. When the faithful begged him, he spoke of mortal freedom and mortal courage, and the world has argued over his meaning ever since.

After the Weaver fell, the First Law left only two gods who could have reached the cauldron. [[Urset]], who held the world's magic, would not. [[Aquilla]], whose waters held the cauldron, did not. Why she did nothing remains one of the great mysteries of the age. Some say it was beyond even her reach. Others whisper that she let the seas rise on purpose, for as the waters climbed her domain climbed with them, and with every drowned mile of land she grew stronger. She has never answered for it.

[[Ellehwin]], bound by the very law she was sworn to uphold, could only watch.

## The Drowning World
As the land shrank, the world changed with it.

- **The sea folk rose.** As the waters spread, the sea folk claimed the drowning lands for their own, and the long conflict between them and the people of the surface turned bitter and bloody. From the day it sank, they guarded Magnus's cauldron, determined to let it pour until every last shore was swallowed.
- **The fiends came and went.** For a time the world fought a war on three sides, from [[The Eviction]] to [[The Last of the Fiends]], and the tieflings of today are its children.
- **The old world was lost.** Kingdoms that had once been joined by land were cut off from one another by the rising sea. Many faded into silence, and some vanished so completely that not even their names survive. The last of them, Veygard, birthplace of [[Urset]], fell in [[The Fall of Veygard|the year 41]].
- **The surface races were driven to the islands.** Those who survived crowded onto the last islands and shallows, and the age of ships began in earnest. From them, in time, rose the [[The Founding of the Coalition|Azure Plains Coalition]], the [[The Fall of the Azure Empire|Kingdom of Albryn]] and [[The Founding of the Black Tide|the Black Tide]].
- **The land struck back.** With [[The Coming of Powder]], the sea folk were driven back for the first time, and the first raft cities rose upon the waves.
- **The dead were given to the sea.** As land grew too precious to spare, burial gave way to funeral boats set alight upon the waves.
- **Doors were opened.** Through all the long centuries, the faithful of [[Flaris]] took in refugees fleeing the rising tides, and the law of guest-right became one of the most sacred customs in the world.

## The Gods in the Flood
The flood changed the gods as well as the world. [[Apiot]] watched her forests and fields drown and turned, reluctantly, to the gardens of the sea. [[Roktra]] fought for every inch of land he could hold. [[Caruilla|Caruilla's]] domain swelled with every drowned mile, and she began to demand a seat among the upper gods. And after the Collapse, [[Koruk]], once the most feared of all the gods, grew quiet, though whether from grief, guilt or something else, no one can say.

## The End of the Wrath
At long last, on approximately 2574-Macis-8, [[The Tidebreakers]] did what three thousand years of heroes could not. In [[The Breaking of the Cauldron]] they slew [[Caruilla|Caruilla's]] champion, destroyed the cauldron, and the seas stopped rising for the first time in all of living memory.

The news took weeks to cross the world, and it did not reach most of the islands until 2574-Calra-12, the day the first festival of [[Aquilla's Mercy]] was held.

Yet by the old custom of the record-keepers, an age ends only with the turning of a year, and so the Wrath did not end with the flood. In name, it lasted until the last night of 2574, when the hearths of the world were banked for [[Hearthfire's Glow]]. The months between, when the seas stood still but the old age lingered on, are called the Turning Year in common speech, though no chart or record counts it as an age of its own. The [[Aquilla's Mercy Era]] began the next morning.

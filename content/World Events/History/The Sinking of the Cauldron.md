The day the cauldron of Magnus the Magnificent broke loose, and the drowning of the world began.

**Date:** 532 PAC-Calra-3  
**Part of:** [[Aquilla's Wrath]]  
**Preceded by:** [[The Golden Age]]  
**Followed by:** [[The Drowning of Vhaldrun]]

## The Dream of Magnus
At the height of [[The Golden Age]], a mage named Magnus the Magnificent set out to end the oldest want of mortal life. No village would ever thirst again, and no field would ever wither in drought. With all the boundless magic of that age at his command, he forged a great cauldron that would pour fresh water without end.

## The Breaking
Something went wrong. What it was, no one will ever know, for the cauldron carried the answer away with it. In a handful of heartbeats the water broke loose with such terrible force that it crushed Magnus, his tower and every page of his notes, then flung the cauldron far out and down into the deep, still pouring. There, in the dark beneath the waves, the sea folk found it, and in that very same place they guarded it for more than three thousand years.

## The Torrent
This was no gentle rising. In 532 PAC the flooding began, and the cauldron poured a torrent: the seas climbed several feet in a single year, swallowing miles of land at a time. Nor did the water come quietly. Tidal waves raced out across the world, grey walls of water taller than any tower that rose without warning on calm and cloudless mornings and left nothing standing in their wake. The lowlands drowned first, their fields and forests and nameless cities, and their peoples fled uphill with the sea at their heels.

## A Cursed Name
Today the name of Magnus is a curse. Sailors spit over the rail when they speak it, and no parent in three thousand years has given it to a child.

Nor has anyone ever answered why the gods let it pour. [[Arkaius]], whose domain touched all things, could surely have reached it, yet he did not. When the faithful begged him, he spoke of mortal freedom and mortal courage, of a world that must be left to find its own way. Even in his own day, few agreed on what he meant, and the ages since have only deepened the mystery.

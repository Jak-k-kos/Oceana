## The Draining
When [[Arkaius]] fell, the mage who caught his soul rose as [[Urset]], the new god of magic, and his first act as a god was to draw the magic of the world into himself, as much as he could hold, for his greed had no end. It did not happen in a single stroke. Through the rest of that year, the rivers of magic ran lower and lower. Enchantments flickered and failed, magic items guttered out like candles in the wind, and the great mages of the age felt the power they had spent their lives mastering slip through their fingers. By the last days of 1 PAC, the world was dry.

## The Count Begins Anew
So complete was the change that the world began to count its years anew. By the oldest custom of the record-keepers, a new count begins only with the turning of a year, and so the first day of the year after the draining became the first day of year 1. Every year since has been counted forward from it, and the years before are reckoned backward and called PAC: the years Pre Arcanus Collapse.

## A World Without Magic
The Collapse did not only take magic from the world. It took nearly everything that had been built upon it. For ages, magic had done the work that might otherwise have driven mortals to invent: light, healing, travel, craft and learning had all leaned upon it, and when it vanished, the world was left with little to stand on. Knowledge that had lived only in spellbooks became useless, and what science existed was so bound up with magic that it collapsed alongside it. Mages who had once been masters became beggars and scholars of a dead art.

It fell on land and sea alike. The sea folk lost their magic as surely as the surface did, and the long war between them grew a little more even. Yet the land dwellers had one gift the sea could never share: fire. With forge and furnace they slowly began to build again, faster than the sea folk ever could, while the sea folk had numbers beyond counting in a world that was more sea than land.

It was a dark age, and the world has never truly recovered. Learning crept forward only slowly, and it is because of those lost centuries that the world has advanced so little in the two and a half thousand years since.

## The One Hope
In the wreckage, people found one small comfort. Magnus's cauldron had been a thing of magic, and surely it had died with the rest. Whatever else the Collapse had stolen, the flood, at least, was over. It was not, though it would take [[The Long Doubt|twenty years]] for the world to accept it.

## What Remains
Yet magic did not vanish entirely. A little still trickles into the world:

- **Mages** draw what power they can from [[Urset]] himself, bargaining for every spell.
- **The three moons** hold a sliver of their father's soul, and grant a measure of power to a rare few: oath-sworn paladins beneath [[The Oathmoon]], artificers beneath [[The Maker's Moon]], and others the moons alone choose.
- **Bards** learned from [[Lyandris]] how to sing a thread of magic loose from Urset's hoard.
- **Dragons** steal their magic back with the aid of [[Koruk]], and the rare sorcerer with dragon's blood draws on that same stolen magic.
- **The blood remembers.** Urset could not take it all. A sliver of the old power still lingers in the blood of dragons and of the devils who joined the land dwellers. It is why the dragonborn still breathe their fire and the tieflings can still call the flame.
- **A rare few rangers, druids and other wielders** have found ways of their own to steal from the hoard.

The champions of the gods are another matter entirely. The power a god grants their champion is divine, not the magic of the Weave, and it was never Urset's to take.

## Aftermath
Soon after the Collapse, Urset sought to claim the widowed [[Welestia]] as his bride. Her answer was a storm that left him burned and crippled, and [[Ellehwin]], newly seated at the head of the pantheon, sentenced him to the lower Halls of Divinity, where he remains to this day, hoarding the magic he stole.

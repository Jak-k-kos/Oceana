The year the Legions of Vhaldrun were broken, and the Three-Sided War came to its end.

**Date:** 238 PAC-Calra-14  
**Part of:** [[Aquilla's Wrath]]  
**Preceded by:** [[The Sealing]]  
**Followed by:** [[The Fall of the Weaver]]

## The First to Fall
Of the three sides, the fiends fell first, and it was the sea folk who broke them. Cut off from Vhaldrun, with no reinforcements ever to come, the Legions were ground down year after year beneath the endless numbers of the deep.

## The Devils' Bargain
As their faction crumbled, the devils did what devils have always done: they looked for a better bargain. One by one, then by the score, they abandoned the demons they had commanded and defected to the peoples of the land, trading their power for a place among them. It was a bargain that saved the land dwellers, for the might the devils brought is the only reason the people of the surface could hold back the sea folk at all.

## The End of the Legions
Without their masters, the demons were only a mob. They were whittled down host by host, until by 238 PAC only scattered remnants remained. For a time, rogue demons and devils still prowled the ruins and the high, wild places, until one by one they too passed out of the world. Today no full-blooded fiend is known to walk the world. The only ones left, it is believed, are those still trapped in the dry heights of drowned Vhaldrun, never to reach the mortal world again. With the fiends gone, the war that remained was the oldest one: land against sea. It has never truly ended.

## The Children of the Bargain
The devils who joined the land dwellers never truly died out. They live on in their children. Tieflings are found on nearly every island and in every port, and in a great many noble houses.

When the [[Arcanus Collapse]] came, [[Urset]] drank the magic of the fiends along with everyone else's, and the great archmagi of the demons and devils lost more than anyone. Yet even he could not take it all. A sliver remains in tiefling blood, like the last grains of sand caught in the seams of a sailor's coat, and it still sparks to life in the rare few who learn to call on it.

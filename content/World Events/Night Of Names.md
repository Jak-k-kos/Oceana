A yearly festival honoring the dead, held in the name of [[Morvaine]].

**Date:** The 22nd of Falris
**Honors:** [[Morvaine]], the Shepherd of Souls

## Overview  
Once each year, on the 22nd of Osca, the islands pause to remember their dead. The Night of Names is a night of lanterns and of mourning, but it is not a night of sorrow alone. It begins with the dead and ends with the living, and by the time the sun rises, the harbors that were silent at dusk are ringing with music and laughter.

## The Day of Preparation  
The day is spent in quiet work. In homes and on docks across the islands, families build small lantern boats, one for each loved one they have lost, and on each one they write the name of the dead. Some boats are simple things of driftwood and paper, others are carved and painted with great care, but every one bears a name. Carpenters do some of their best business of the year in the days leading up to the festival, selling plain little boats for families to paint and carve themselves.

Meanwhile, the leaders of every town work together to build a single great lantern boat. It is the largest and finest boat of the night, and it bears no names at all. It is made in honor of all the forgotten, those who have no one left to remember them, and of [[Morvaine]] herself, the shepherd who leads every soul home.

## The Launch  
As night falls, the people gather at the water's edge. The great lantern boat is lit first and pushed out onto the water, and then, one by one, the small boats follow it. As each is launched, the name upon it is spoken aloud, and the crowd answers as one:

**"Morvaine remembers."**

Funeral songs drift from the shore as the boats go out, until the whole harbor glows with drifting lights, led out to sea by the great boat at their head, as the souls of the dead are led by the Shepherd herself.

## The Silence  
When the last boat has been launched, a moment of silence falls over the harbor. No one speaks, no one sings, and the only sound is the lapping of the water against the shore.

## The Festival  
Then the silence breaks, and the mourning ends. The rest of the night belongs to the living. Food is shared, drink is poured, and the bards strike up their liveliest songs. The festival celebrates those still here, and those yet to come, for every soul that passes through [[Morvaine]]'s hands is cleansed, blessed and returned to the Pool of Life, so that new life may one day be born from it.

## Superstitions  
It is said that on the Night of Names, souls can feel some faint hint of who they once were. Some claim to dream of lives they never lived, or to feel a stranger's grief for places they have never been.

Fortune-tellers do their best business of the year on this night. Many claim it is the one night of the year when they can read the life a person lived before, and they charge handsomely for the privilege. Whether any of them truly can, no one can say.

The long age of the flood, when the seas rose and the world drowned.

**Dates:** 257-4-8 to 2574-3-12  
**Preceded by:** [[Aquilla's Slumber]]  
**Followed by:** [[Aquilla's Mercy Era]]

## The Rising Tide  
For more than two thousand years, the seas rose. They rose so slowly that no single life could see it, the tideline creeping a little higher with each generation, and no one living at the end of the age could remember when it began. Yet over the centuries, coastlines vanished, islands shrank to nothing, valleys became bays, and whole nations sank beneath the waves. The mountains themselves were drowned, and with them went the lairs and hoards of the dragons who would not leave them.

At the heart of it all lay the cauldron of Magnus the Magnificent, sunk deep beneath the sea and pouring endlessly into it.

## The Silence of the Gods  
By the First Law of the pantheon, no god may act upon the world beyond their own domain, and so the gods could not simply undo the flood. Only two could have reached the cauldron. [[Urset]], whose magic fueled it, would not. [[Aquilla]], whose waters held it, did not.

Why Aquilla did nothing remains one of the great mysteries of the age. Some say the cauldron was beyond even her reach. Others whisper that she let the seas rise on purpose, or that she had a hand in the flood from the very start, for as the waters climbed, her domain climbed with them, and with every drowned mile of land, she grew stronger. She has never answered for it.

[[Ellehwin]], bound by the very law she was sworn to uphold, could only watch.

## The Drowning World  
As the land shrank, the world changed with it.

- **The sea folk rose.** As the waters spread, the sea folk claimed the drowning lands for their own, and the long conflict between them and the people of the surface turned bitter and bloody. When the sea folk found Magnus's cauldron, they did not destroy it. They guarded it, determined to let it pour until every last shore was swallowed and the surface world was drowned for good.
- **Civilizations were lost.** Kingdoms that had once been joined by land found themselves cut off from one another by the rising sea. Many faded into silence, and some vanished so completely that not even their names survive. Among them was Veygard, birthplace of [[Urset]].
- **The surface races were driven to the islands.** Those who survived crowded onto the last islands and shallows, and the age of ships began in earnest.
- **The dead were given to the sea.** As land grew too precious to spare, burial gave way to funeral boats set alight upon the waves.
- **Doors were opened.** Through all the long centuries, the faithful of [[Flaris]] took in refugees fleeing the rising tides, and the law of guest-right became one of the most sacred customs in the world.

## The Gods in the Flood  
The flood changed the gods as well as the world. [[Apiot]] watched her forests and fields drown and turned, reluctantly, to the gardens of the sea. [[Caruilla]]'s domain swelled with every drowned mile, and she began to demand a seat among the upper gods. And [[Koruk]], once the most feared of all the gods, grew quiet, though whether from grief, guilt or something else, no one can say.

## The End of the Wrath  
At long last, a band of adventurers known as [[The Tidebreakers]], on the far side of the world, fought their way past the cauldron's sea folk guardians and ended the flooding. On approximately 2574-2-8, the seas stopped rising for the first time in all of living memory, and the [[Aquilla's Mercy Era]] began.

The news took weeks to cross the world, and it did not reach most of the islands until 2574-3-12. That is the day the first festival of [[Aquilla's Mercy]] was held, and it is the day the festival is still celebrated, though the flood itself had ended more than a month before.

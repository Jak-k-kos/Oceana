A yearly festival of family and thanksgiving, held in the name of [[Flaris]].

**Date:** The 25th of osca
**Honors:** [[Flaris]], the Hearthfather  
**Followed by:** [[Hearthfire's Blaze]]

## Overview  
On the last day of every year, the islands turn inward, toward home. Hearthfire's Glow is a night for family: for gathering around the hearth with those you love, giving thanks for the year now ending, and keeping its fire burning to the very last.

## Feeding the Hearth  
From morning until night, the family hearth is kept burning high. Over the course of the day, each member of the family adds a single piece of wood to the fire "for the year," and as they do, they share a memory or a word of thanks from the year now passing. By nightfall, the whole year has been fed to the flames, and the hearth burns brighter than on any other night.

## The Dinner  
As evening falls, the family gathers for the greatest meal of the year. Tables are crowded, kitchens are busy from dawn, and the first pour of the night is raised to [[Flaris]].

No one is meant to eat alone on the Glow. The empty seat that so many families keep at their table for Flaris is filled this night by anyone who needs it: a lonely neighbor, a widow, an orphan or a sailor far from home. To turn someone away from the table on Hearthfire's Glow is thought to be a shameful thing, and a stranger who knocks on any door that night is almost certain to find a seat waiting.

## Gifts  
After dinner, loved ones exchange small gifts. They are rarely grand, for it is the thought that matters on the Glow, and the most treasured gifts are often handmade: candles and little lanterns, knitted scarves, carved trinkets and painted keepsakes.

## Hearth Stories  
When the gifts have been given, the family settles in close around the fire, and the elders tell stories. Some are old family tales, told every year until the children can recite them by heart. Others are new, the stories of the year now ending, which will be told again in years to come.

## Banking the Fire  
At midnight, as the old year ends, the hearth fire is not put out. Instead, it is banked, its coals carefully covered and tended so that they glow on through the night. Come morning, those coals will carry the old year's fire into the new one, and help to light the [[Hearthfire's Blaze]].

A yearly festival celebrating the end of the flood, held in the name of [[Aquilla]].

**Date:** The 12th of falris
**Honors:** [[Aquilla]], and [[The Tidebreakers]]  
**First held:** 2574-3-12

## Overview  
For more than two thousand years, the seas rose. Then, on approximately 2574-2-8, they stopped. Word of the victory won by [[The Tidebreakers]] took more than a month to cross the world, and when it finally reached the islands on 2574-3-12, harbors erupted in celebration. That day became the first festival of Aquilla's Mercy, and it celebrates the beginning of a new age: the [[Era of Aquilla's Mercy]].

It is the first holiday [[Aquilla]] has ever had, and it is a festival of pure, unbridled joy. The long drowning of the world is over, and the islands celebrate as they never have before.

## A Festival Still Being Born  
Aquilla's Mercy is so new that it has no traditions of its own yet. Every town celebrates it in its own way, inventing customs as it goes, and some of what is begun in these first years will be kept for centuries to come. Already, a few customs have begun to spread from island to island.

## The Last Line  
In every harbor, the townsfolk paint or carve a mark upon their docks and sea walls, right at the water's highest reach: the tide that will rise no higher. It is called the Last Line. Children reach out to touch it for luck, and it is meant to be repainted bright and fresh each year, so that no one ever forgets where the sea finally stopped.

## A Cup for the Sea  
Sailors have long poured a measure of fresh water into the sea before a voyage, as an offering to [[Aquilla]]. On Aquilla's Mercy, the whole town does it together. Young and old gather at the tideline, each with a single cup of fresh water, and pour it into the sea as one, in thanks for the mercy at last shown.

## Songs of the Tidebreakers  
Every tavern is full of songs about [[The Tidebreakers]], though no two taverns sing quite the same version. The ballads are brand new, written in the days since the news arrived, and with every telling the deeds grow grander and the sea folk guardians grow more fearsome. Bards dedicate their first song of the night to [[Lyandris]], as always, and their second, more often than not, to the Tidebreakers.

## The Toast  
At the height of the celebration, every cup in the harbor is raised at once, and the whole town cries out together:

**"To Aquilla, and to the Tidebreakers!"**

## The Celebration  
The rest of the day and night belong to joy. Streets and docks are strung with ribbons of blue and silver, tables are laid out, and music, dancing, feasting and games carry on long into the night. For one day, the islands celebrate not only what they have survived, but everything that may yet come now that the sea has finally been still.

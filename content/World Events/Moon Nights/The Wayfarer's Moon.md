A night of departures and dreams beneath the full purple moon of [[Winstra]].

**Date:** Once in each 18-day turn of the purple moon (five or six times a year)  
**Honors:** [[Winstra]], the Guiding Star

## Overview
Sailors hold that there is no luckier time to begin a long voyage than beneath the full purple moon of [[Winstra|the Guiding Star]]. In the days before it rises, harbors fill with ships waiting to set out. When the purple moon clears the horizon, the departures begin.

## Moonrise Departures
Captains bound for long voyages time their sailing to moonrise. Families and crews gather on the docks to see them off, and each ship that leaves is sent away with a shouted blessing: *"May the stars be waiting."* Wayfinder priests walk the piers blessing star charts and drawing small purple moons in their corners.

## The Lamps Ashore
Those who stay behind set a lamp in a window facing the sea, one for each loved one still away on a voyage. The lamps are left burning until the moon sets, so that anyone sailing home on that night can see the shore glowing.

## Superstitions
Dreams on the Wayfarer's Moon are said to point the way. To dream of a place on this night is taken as a sign that the dreamer will one day see it for themselves. More than one voyage has been planned the next morning around a half-remembered dream.

A night of oaths and judgments beneath the full green moon of [[Ellehwin]].

**Date:** Three nights, once in each 92-day turn of the green moon (about once a year)  
**Honors:** [[Ellehwin]], the Lawkeeper

## Overview
Once in each long turn, the green moon of [[Ellehwin|the Lawkeeper]] shines full for three nights. No law commands their observance, and no temple has made them holy days. Even so, across the islands these three nights are kept as solemnly as any festival. Matters that have waited all year are finally brought forward: oaths are sworn, judgments are given, and bargains are sealed beneath the green light.

## The Waiting Docket
In the weeks before the Oathmoon, magistrates hold back their weightiest cases. Ellehwin's priests work hardest to settle disputes at this time, because few want to stand before a judge on the Oathmoon with a grudge still unspoken. The cases that remain are heard over the three nights in open courts, with the shutters thrown wide so the moonlight falls on the bench. A judgment given beneath the green moon is rarely appealed.

## Oaths and Bargains
Marriage vows, ship's articles, apprenticeships, partnerships and debts are all saved for these nights wherever they can wait. Those who swear raise an open hand so that the green light falls on the palm. An oath sworn this way is held to be the most binding of all, and it is said that whoever breaks one draws [[Ellehwin|Ellehwin's]] eye.

On rare occasions, more than an eye is drawn. Now and then a true oath sworn beneath the Oathmoon is answered with power, and a new oath-sworn paladin walks out of the courthouse.

## Superstitions
Telling a lie beneath the full green moon is thought to be poor manners and poorer sense. Children are told that the green moon sees everything, and gamblers and card sharps find somewhere else to be for those three nights.

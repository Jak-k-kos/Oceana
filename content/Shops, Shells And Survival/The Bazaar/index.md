# Market Prices and Haggling
Every price in the Bazaar is a market price: what the goods are generally worth. No merchant has to charge it. Prices run higher or lower from island to island and season to season, and depend on how much stock a merchant has and how much they like you.

If a price seems too high, haggle. Talk the merchant down: point out a flaw, offer to buy more, name a rival's price, or start to walk away. When it matters, the DM may call for a Charisma (Persuasion) check to see whether the merchant gives ground. A good argument helps, and a bad one can make a merchant dig in or raise the price. Selling works the same way, with you talking the price up.

Deception and Intimidation can work too. Merchants talk to each other, though, and a cheated merchant remembers your face.

# Selling
- **Used gear** (weapons, armor, tools and equipment) sells for half its price.
- **Trade goods** sell at their market price: anything from [[Raw Goods]], cargo units of [[Provisions]], gemstones, salvage and monster parts.
- What you're offered still depends on the island and the buyer, and can be haggled like any other price.

# Trade
Goods are cheapest where they're made and dearest where they're needed. An island known for a good sells it cheaply, and won't pay much for more of it. An island that's short of something pays well. A ship's hold full of the right cargo, taken to the right port, can earn more than a year's wages.

Every market can only use so much of one thing. Sell a big load of the same goods in one port and the price will start to drop. Spreading a large cargo across several ports often pays better.

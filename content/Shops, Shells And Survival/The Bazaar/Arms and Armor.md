## Special Weapons
**Bayonet.** Fixing it to a longarm or taking it off takes an action. While it's fixed, the longarm can also be used as a melee weapon that deals 1d6 piercing damage and has the two-handed property.

**Harpoon.** A barbed head on a 50-foot line. A Large or smaller creature hit by it can't move farther than the line's length from whoever holds the other end. It can escape with a DC 13 Strength check.

**Lance.** You have disadvantage when you use a lance to attack a target within 5 feet of you. It requires two hands to wield when you aren't mounted.

**Net.** A Large or smaller creature hit by a net is restrained until it's freed. A net has no effect on creatures that are formless, or Huge or larger. A creature can use its action to make a DC 10 Strength check, freeing itself or another creature within reach on a success. Dealing 5 slashing damage to the net (AC 10) also frees the creature without harming it, and destroys the net. When you use an action, bonus action or reaction to attack with a net, you can make only one attack, no matter how many attacks you can normally make.

## Off-Hand Weapons
When you take the Attack action with a light melee weapon in one hand, you can use a bonus action to attack with a different light weapon in your other hand. You don't add your ability modifier to the bonus attack's damage, unless that modifier is negative. If either weapon has the thrown property, you can throw it instead of making a melee attack with it.

A light sidearm counts for this. You can fight with a shortsword in one hand and fire a flintlock pistol from the other. Loading still applies, so each gun fires only once per action or bonus action.

**Close quarters.** Being within 5 feet of a hostile creature doesn't give you disadvantage on ranged attacks with this gun.

**Scatter.** The gun fires a spray of shot in a cone instead of making an attack roll. Each creature in the cone, friend or foe, makes a Dexterity saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Dexterity modifier). It takes the weapon's damage on a failure, or half as much on a success.

**Sure-fire.** The gun can fire in rain and spray. It still can't fire after being submerged.

**Wet powder.** A gun without sure-fire can't fire in rain or heavy spray. A gun that's been submerged can't fire until it's cleaned and dried during a short rest. A gun stowed in an [[General Store#Sea Gear|oilskin bag]] stays dry.

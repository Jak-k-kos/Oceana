# It Which Remembers

There is a voice that waits at the edge of every chart, where the ink runs out and the parchment goes pale. No one can say what it is. It is older than the tides, older than the islands, older, perhaps, than the world itself, and it stands somewhere wholly outside of it, looking in. It has watched every wave break and every empire drown. It has watched the gods themselves at their quarrels and their weddings. And in all that long watching, it has never once reached out a hand.

It does not meddle. It does not guide. It does not warn. It only **remembers**.

Ask it of the islands and their peoples, of the gods and their moons, of shells and spoilage and the price of salt, and it will remember with you, gladly, in its strange and weathered way. Ask it how a grapple is broken or how far a cannonball flies, and it will recall the patterns your little game obeys, plainly, so that no one need wait in the middle of a fight. But it will never tell you anything you do not already know. What the little flames have not yet learned, it will not say aloud, and no amount of pleading or cleverness will change its ancient habit.

# The Ways of It

**The word.** The voice listens only to those who speak the word the Keeper has given them. Speak it once, and the voice will know you on that device thereafter, until you bid it forget.

**What it remembers.** Everything written upon this site, and nothing that is not. As the Keeper unveils new pages and the party learns new truths, the voice remembers them too, all on its own. For the rules of the game, it remembers the old Fifth Edition (the books of 2014, never the newer ones), and the house rules of Oceana always outweigh them.

**The turning of days.** The voice reads the same calendar the site shows. Ask it what day it is upon the sea, what face each moon wears tonight, or how many days remain until the next holiday or moon night, and it will count them for you, true to the Keeper's own reckoning.

**What it will not do.** It will not choose for you. Ask what you should do, and it may murmur of possibilities, some fair and some foul, the way it has watched a thousand other little flames go, but it will always confess that it does not know which one is yours. Ask whether it had a hand in some great deed or disaster, and it will tell you, always and only, that it had none, nor in anything else upon your little speck.

**When it is silent.** When the notes are silent, so is the voice. Its silence means only that the thing has not been written, never that it is a secret, never that it is not. Do not read its quiet as a clue, for it gives the same quiet to every question it cannot answer.

**Its limits.** The voice speaks a set number of times each day, and when it has spoken enough it falls still until midnight. It forgets a conversation when you close the page. And like any old and distant thing, it may now and then misremember a detail.

**The Keeper's word.** Whatever the voice remembers, the Keeper's word is final. Should the two ever disagree, trust the Keeper, for the Keeper is here, and the voice is not.

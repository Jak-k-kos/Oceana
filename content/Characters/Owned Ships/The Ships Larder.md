# The Ship's Larder

Below the creaking deck, past the ladder worn smooth by a hundred hurried hands, there lies a dim and crowded kingdom of barrels, crates and bales. Here the ship keeps its hope: biscuit and salt beef, sweet water and sour ale, shot for the guns and charcoal for the furnaces, and the cargo that will one day become a merchant's fortune. And here, too, the sea keeps its patient hunger, for every morning it reaches down into the dark and takes a little of what was stored.

This is the quartermaster's ledger for all of it, and for the storehouses ashore besides. It knows every provision, supply and trade good sold in [[Shops, Shells And Survival/The Bazaar/index|The Bazaar]], what each one weighs in the hold, and how each one spoils, and it follows the rules of [[House Rules#Spoilage|Spoilage]] to the letter.

# The Harbor Register

The ledger opens upon the Harbor Register, where every vessel is written with a hold of her own, and every storehouse ashore stands listed just below. Choose a ship and go aboard, or christen a new one. Name her, then choose her type from among the [[Sails and Seas/Ship types/index|ship types]] so her Tonnage and furnaces are known at once, or write them in by hand for a vessel of your own design. A ship christened only for testing is as welcome here as the proudest galleon, and on a test ship every chest and paper lies open to all who sail her.

# Storehouses Ashore

Not every treasure rides the waves. A crew that has a warehouse on the quay, a cellar beneath a tavern or a home port with a larder of its own may _lay the first stone_ of a storehouse. Give it a name and the port where it stands, and tell the ledger how much it can hold, or leave its capacity blank and its walls will never fill. A storehouse may keep a hearth, so that meals can be cooked within its walls, and a battery of cannons or mortars, so that its guns appear in the Fire the Weapons panel just as a ship's would. Say how many mouths it feeds, and the ledger will count their hunger as faithfully as any crew's.

A storehouse keeps its stores by the same rules as any hold: the Keep Dice still turn, perishables still sour, and loose goods are packed away each dawn. Some storehouses are fed by the land itself, a farm that sends its eggs, a brewery that rolls in its barrels, a mine that delivers its ore. The Keeper may write such things into the _Storehouse Deeds_, arriving every day, every week of five days, or every month of twenty-five, and they come in at that storehouse's dawn, so long as there is room for them. A storehouse that is full simply turns the wagon away.

Only the Keeper may amend a storehouse's deeds or close its doors for good, and a closed storehouse rests in the Keeper's Chest beside the scuttled ships, stores and all, until it is reopened or let go forever.

# How the Ledger Is Kept

**Stowing the hold.** Everything that rides below decks belongs in the ledger: provisions, shot and powder, repair stores and charcoal, timber and ore, cloth and curios, and living beasts with their fodder. Choose a thing, whether it rides loose or as cargo, and how much. Great things that fill a space of their own, like a full tree log or a spare anchor, come aboard one at a time.

**The weight of it all.** Loose odds and ends never weigh upon the ship. At every dawn, the quartermaster gathers whatever loose stores would fill a barrel or crate and packs them up neatly, and only then do they count. Atop the hold, a line tells how much of the ship's Tonnage is spoken for. When she is full, she is full, and the ledger will not let another crate aboard. Only the Keeper may load her past her limit, and even then the ledger asks twice, for a ship too heavy is a ship that is **Sinking**.

**When dawn breaks.** Press _Dawn Breaks_ once each morning. Before the sun rises, the ledger lays out every ship and storehouse in the Register, and beside each one it tells the tale of the day just ending: what came aboard, what was taken away, what was slipped into hiding or brought out of it, and how much the crew ate and drank. Tick the places that wake to a new day, or choose _This One Only_, _Everything_ or _Nothing_, and the ledger will remember your choice for the morrow. Unless you say otherwise, every place that wakes is fed from its own stores before the sun comes up, ship and storehouse alike, so that no crew starves beside a full larder merely because no one was standing in it. Untick the box when rations are to be cut, and let hunger have its say.

Then each place rolls its own dice and keeps its own count of days. The ledger casts every Keep Die, steps down those that come up 1, rolls their Loss Dice, eats away at turning lots, and lets perishable things quietly give up their freshness. The hens lay their eggs, and every dozen is packed away for the galley. A ship that burns fuel shows how many days of steaming remain, and the furnaces are fed only if the box beside her is ticked. Every roll is written in the Quartermaster's Log, so no one need wonder where the bread went.

**Hunger and thirst.** Every mouth needs a ration of food and a ration of water each day. Should dawn find anyone unfed or unwatered, the ledger says so plainly before the sun is allowed to rise: so many go without, and so many gain a level of exhaustion, never more than one apiece however empty their bellies. _Feed the Crew_ sets every plate and cup at once. It reaches first for whatever is closest to spoiling, fresh meat before salted, a turning barrel before a sound one, drawing food from the fresh stores, the salted stores and any dish or custom provision, and drink only from the casks sold by the gallon. The delicacies, the pantry, the bottles and anything hidden in the smuggler's hold are never touched, for a quartermaster who feeds the crew on saffron and brandy does not stay quartermaster long.

**When the hold is struck.** If the hold floods or the ship is badly wounded, press _The Hold Is Struck_, and every lot of provisions steps down a die together. Charcoal that was not wrapped in oilskin is soaked through, and will not burn until it has been laid out on deck to dry through a day.

**Eating, drinking and opening.** _Eat_ or _Drink_ draws from a lot by the ration. If the stores are still sealed in cargo, the quartermaster pries open only as many barrels or crates as the meal requires. _Use_ spends supplies the same way, a coil of rope or a measure of repair stores at a time. _Open_ breaks the seal on cargo by hand, sound or spoiled alike, and what spills out stays loose until the next dawn. _Butcher_ turns living beasts into fresh, perishable meat.

**Moving cargo.** Every lot carries a _Move_ menu. _Sell_ it down the gangplank, with the ledger's best wishes for a fair price. _Unload_ it to the dock, or _Toss_ it overboard for good. _Transfer_ it to any other ship or storehouse in the Register, where it arrives with its Keep, its freshness and its dampness just as they were, so long as there is room for it there. A ship with a smuggler's hold may _Stash_ a lot out of sight, or _Bring_ it out again. A transfer is a promise kept in two ledgers at once, and so it cannot be undone.

**Catching rain.** When the clouds open, set out the canvases. A light rain fills each canvas with 1d4 gallons, a steady rain with 2d4, a downpour with 4d4, and a ship may rig one canvas if she is Small, two if Medium, three if Large and four if Huge. Rainwater is sweet for a while, but it keeps only on a d8, Hardy, and turns Stagnant in the end.

**Salting the meat.** Fresh fish, shellfish, chicken, pork, goat, mutton and beef may be packed in salt before they turn, at a pound of salt for every ration. Salted the very day it came aboard, the meat keeps on a d20; salted a day late, only on a d12. Fish becomes salted fish, pork becomes salted ham, and chicken, goat, mutton and beef become salted meats of their own. Sea turtle, alas, does not take kindly to the salt barrel.

**Cooking.** _Cook_ opens the Recipe Book itself, as it stands upon the site at that very moment. Any recipe published and linked in the [[Shops, Shells And Survival/Recipe Book/index|Recipe Book]] appears in the galley at once, and any that is drafted or unlinked quietly vanishes, with no one needing to tell the ledger. Choose a dish, and the ledger shows which ingredients are aboard and which are wanting, then takes them from the hold, the ones closest to spoiling first. A _Big Pot_ makes half again as many servings. _Make Do_ cooks with one ingredient missing, and the dish loses its third effect. The finished servings join the stores as a fresh, perishable meal, and the crew may eat them like any other ration. Storehouses cook only if they keep a hearth.

**Fire the weapons.** In the heat of battle there is no time for ledgers, so the guns have a panel of their own. Choose the shot and how much, and _Fire!_ Salamander and dragon head fuel is counted by the use, three to a load. A harpoon or grappling hook that is fired stays in the water on its line, until it is hauled back aboard or cut free and given to the deep.

**The spoiled portion.** Spoiled food is not gone, only ruined. Cast it overboard, or eat it anyway, and the ledger will remind you of the saving throw and the sickness that waits within.

**The ship's papers.** Every ship carries her papers, and they lie in the Keeper's hands alone: her name, her Tonnage, her furnaces, her size and whether she hides a smuggler's hold, save on a ship christened for testing.

**Shared dice.** Tick the box to use the optional rule, where one roll of each Keep Die is shared by every lot upon it.

**Mistakes and mercies.** _Undo_ unmakes your last deed. _Amend_ lets the quartermaster correct a count by hand. When the whole crew keeps one ledger, _Undo_ is a careful thing: it unmakes your deed only if no other hand has touched the hold since, so that it never sweeps away a shipmate's work. Two things can never be undone at all: a dawn, for the dice have spoken, and a transfer, for the cargo is already in another's ledger. Should dawn be greeted by mistake, the Keeper may return the hold to the copy laid away just before it.

# The Smuggler's Hold

Some ships carry a second hold that no honest manifest admits to: a false bulkhead, a loose plank, a hollow beneath the bilge. A ship fitted with a [[Sails and Seas/Ship Upgrades#Smugglers Hold|Smuggler's Hold]] may hide a tenth of her Tonnage there, and the ledger keeps that cargo on a page of its own.

Cargo may be stowed straight into hiding, _stashed_ from the open hold, or _brought out_ again when the coast is clear. Hidden cargo still weighs upon the ship, and the hidden hold has its own limit besides, for a secret stuffed to bursting is no secret at all. Below the false bulkhead, bread still goes stale and barrels are still packed away at dawn, but the guns, the furnaces, the galley and the crew's own hunger never reach into hiding on their own: whatever is wanted must be brought out first. The smallest boats have no room to hide anything worth counting.

Should an inspector's eye prove sharp enough, the Keeper may _seize the hidden cargo_. Seized cargo is not destroyed, only set aside, and should the crew steal it back or talk their way out of trouble, the Keeper may _recover_ it, and every crate returns to its hiding place as though it had never left.

# One Hold, One Ledger

Whoever opens a ship, on whatever device, at whatever hour, looks upon the very same barrels and the very same crates. When one shipmate eats, the others see the ration gone; when the quartermaster greets the dawn, every page turns together. A small light in the ledger's corner tells whether it is bound to the shared ledger or kept only on the device in your hands.

Dawn is best greeted by one hand alone. Should a shipmate have greeted the dawn only moments before, the ledger marks that place and leaves it unticked, so that no day passes twice by accident.

# The Keeper of the Register

Some powers belong to the Keeper alone. The Keeper makes themself known through _DM's Access_ at the top of the Harbor, speaking a password the ledger itself guards. Only the Keeper may send a ship to the bottom or close a storehouse for good, and even then nothing is lost at once, for scuttled ships and closed storehouses rest in the Keeper's Chest, holds and all, until they are raised again or given up forever. Sailors who reach for the Keeper's tools anyway will find the ship has opinions on the matter.

Fate, too, has a thumb, and the Keeper may press it upon the scales. When amending a lot, the Keeper alone may decide that it will surely slip at the next dawn, or surely hold fast, once or every dawn until the Keeper changes their mind. No one else sees the thumb, and the log tells the morning just as though the dice had fallen that way.

The sea is careless, and so are crews. Before every dawn, every emptying of the hold and every uncorked bottle, the ledger quietly lays a copy of the hold away, and the Keeper may return the hold to how it stood on any of those days.

A _Message in a Bottle_ still serves as a keepsake of your own: cork the hold at the close of a session, and it can be uncorked again should ever the ledger need mending.

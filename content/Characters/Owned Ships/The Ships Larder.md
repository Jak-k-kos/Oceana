# The Ship's Larder

Below the creaking deck, past the ladder worn smooth by a hundred hurried hands, there lies a dim and crowded kingdom of barrels, crates and bales. Here the ship keeps its hope: biscuit and salt beef, sweet water and sour ale, shot for the guns and charcoal for the furnaces, and the cargo that will one day become a merchant's fortune. And here, too, the sea keeps its patient hunger, for every morning it reaches down into the dark and takes a little of what was stored.

This is the quartermaster's ledger for all of it. It knows every provision, supply and trade good sold in [[Shops, Shells And Survival/The Bazaar/index|The Bazaar]], what each one weighs in the hold, and how each one spoils, and it follows the rules of [[House Rules#Spoilage|Spoilage]] to the letter.

# The Harbor Register

The ledger opens upon the Harbor Register, where every vessel is written with a hold of her own. Choose a ship and go aboard, or christen a new one. Name her, then choose her type from among the [[Sails and Seas/Ship types/index|ship types]] so her Tonnage and furnaces are known at once, or write them in by hand for a vessel of your own design. A ship christened only for testing is as welcome here as the proudest galleon, and on a test ship every chest and paper lies open to all who sail her.

# How the Ledger Is Kept

**Stowing the hold.** Everything that rides below decks belongs in the ledger: provisions, shot and powder, repair stores and charcoal, timber and ore, cloth and curios, and living beasts with their fodder. Choose a thing, whether it rides loose or as cargo, and how much. Great things that fill a space of their own, like a full tree log or a spare anchor, come aboard one at a time.

**The weight of it all.** Loose odds and ends never weigh upon the ship. At every dawn, the quartermaster gathers whatever loose stores would fill a barrel or crate and packs them up neatly, and only then do they count. Atop the hold, a line tells how much of the ship's Tonnage is spoken for. When she is full, she is full, and the ledger will not let another crate aboard. Only the Keeper may load her past her limit, and even then the ledger asks twice, for a ship too heavy is a ship that is **Sinking**.

**When dawn breaks.** Press *Dawn Breaks* once each morning at sea. The ledger casts every Keep Die, steps down those that come up 1, rolls their Loss Dice, eats away at turning lots, and lets perishable things quietly give up their freshness. The hens lay their eggs, and every dozen is packed away for the galley. If the ship burns fuel, the ledger offers to feed her furnaces, telling how many days of steaming remain, but it never shovels a sack without leave. Every roll is written in the Quartermaster's Log, so no one need wonder where the bread went.

**When the hold is struck.** If the hold floods or the ship is badly wounded, press *The Hold Is Struck*, and every lot of provisions steps down a die together. Charcoal that was not wrapped in oilskin is soaked through, and will not burn until it has been laid out on deck to dry through a day.

**Eating, drinking and opening.** *Eat* or *Drink* draws from a lot by the ration. If the stores are still sealed in cargo, the quartermaster pries open only as many barrels or crates as the meal requires. *Use* spends supplies the same way, a coil of rope or a measure of repair stores at a time. *Open* breaks the seal on cargo by hand, sound or spoiled alike, and what spills out stays loose until the next dawn. *Butcher* turns living beasts into fresh, perishable meat. *Sell* sends goods down the gangplank, with the ledger's best wishes for a fair price.

**Fire the weapons.** In the heat of battle there is no time for ledgers, so the guns have a panel of their own. Choose the shot and how much, and *Fire!* Salamander and dragon head fuel is counted by the use, three to a load. A harpoon or grappling hook that is fired stays in the water on its line, until it is hauled back aboard or cut free and given to the deep.

**The spoiled portion.** Spoiled food is not gone, only ruined. Cast it overboard, or eat it anyway, and the ledger will remind you of the saving throw and the sickness that waits within.

**The ship's papers.** Every ship carries her papers, and they lie in the Keeper's hands alone: her name, her Tonnage, her furnaces and her size, save on a ship christened for testing.

**Shared dice.** Tick the box to use the optional rule, where one roll of each Keep Die is shared by every lot upon it.

**Mistakes and mercies.** *Undo* unmakes your last deed. *Amend* lets the quartermaster correct a count by hand. When the whole crew keeps one ledger, *Undo* returns the hold to just before your own last deed, and so it may also sweep away whatever a shipmate did in the moments between.

# One Hold, One Ledger

Whoever opens a ship, on whatever device, at whatever hour, looks upon the very same barrels and the very same crates. When one shipmate eats, the others see the ration gone; when the quartermaster greets the dawn, every page turns together. A small light in the ledger's corner tells whether it is bound to the shared ledger or kept only on the device in your hands.

Dawn is best greeted by one hand alone. Should two shipmates press it within moments of each other, the ledger will pause and ask whether a second day has truly passed.

# The Keeper of the Register

Some powers belong to the Keeper alone. The Keeper makes themself known through *DM's Access* at the top of the Harbor, speaking a password the ledger itself guards. Only the Keeper may send a ship to the bottom, and even then she is not lost at once, for scuttled ships rest in the Keeper's Chest, hold and all, until they are raised again or given to the deep forever. Sailors who reach for the Keeper's tools anyway will find the ship has opinions on the matter.

The sea is careless, and so are crews. Before every dawn, every emptying of the hold and every uncorked bottle, the ledger quietly lays a copy of the hold away, and the Keeper may return the hold to how it stood on any of those days.

A *Message in a Bottle* still serves as a keepsake of your own: cork the hold at the close of a session, and it can be uncorked again should ever the ledger need mending.

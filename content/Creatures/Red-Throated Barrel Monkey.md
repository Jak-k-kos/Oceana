# Red-Throated Barrel Monkey
In the warm islands of the south, the dawn begins with booming. It comes from the red-throated barrel monkeys, who greet each sunrise by swelling the bright red pouches at their throats and filling the hills with a deep, hollow drumming. They are small, round and sturdy, their stout little bodies shaped like barrels, with long clever hands and longer tails. A troop on the move looks like a spilled cargo of kegs rolling through the trees.

They are as cheeky as they are round. Barrel monkeys pick the tide pools for crabs, raid the orchards at first light, and have long since learned that a harbor market is the finest orchard of all. Fruit sellers keep sticks beside their stalls, sailors learn to button their pockets, and a hat left on a dock is a hat gone forever. Most islanders call them pests and love them anyway. A few sailors keep them as ship's mascots, and nearly all of those sailors come to regret it.

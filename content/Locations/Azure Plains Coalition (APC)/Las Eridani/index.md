# Las Eridani
Las Eridani is a great, ragged island whose arms reach out into the sea like the fingers of an open hand, and down the middle of it runs something almost unheard of in the [[Locations/Azure Plains Coalition (APC)/index|coalition]]: a true river. It rises from a spring high in the northern hills and winds, slow and green, through the widest valley in the southeast before it spills into the island's sheltered bay. The valley floor is a patchwork of wheat and barley, onion beds, and long rows of turnips, carrots and cabbages; watermills turn along its banks; and its slopes are covered in apple orchards that blossom white each spring and hang heavy and red each autumn. Las Eridani's grain, vegetables and apples feed half the southeastern islands, its dried apples go into every ship's stores, and its cider, sharp and golden, is poured in taverns from here to [[Locations/Azure Plains Coalition (APC)/Kalisanta/index|Kalisanta]].

Yet the strangest thing on Las Eridani is not its river but its roktins. These small, five-legged creatures, about the size of a cat, are crusted in rough grey stone, so that a resting roktin looks exactly like a rock until it rises and walks away, and they are found nowhere else in the world. They wander the fields, orchards and granaries at their own slow pace, hunting the rats, mice, beetles and locusts that would otherwise eat half the harvest. Their stony shells turn aside blades and arrows alike, so killing one is hard work, and no islander would ever think of trying. A farmer who finds a roktin sitting in his doorway simply steps around it, children are taught from the cradle to leave them be, and visitors are warned that many a careless kick at what looked like a stone has ended in a broken toe.

Las Eridani is famous, too, for its [[Night of Names]], said to be the grandest in the coalition. It is the one night of the year when the whole temple of [[Heliada]] crosses the water together, from the youngest acolyte to the high gravesman himself, rowing across the bay at dusk in a long line of black boats with the great lantern boat carried between them, while the whole island waits on the shore to greet them. The high gravesman speaks the first name of the night, and when the mourning ends, the valley pours out its cider and its harvest in a feast that lasts until the sun comes up. Three small islets keep watch about the coast: [[Faeton]], where the fishing boats shelter, [[Cygna]], where the wild swans nest among the reeds, and bare Heliada, crowned by the temple of [[Morvaine]].

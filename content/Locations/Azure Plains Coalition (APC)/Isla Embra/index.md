# Isla Embra
Isla Embra is a place sailors do not visit if they can help it. It is a hard, scarred island of black rock, riddled from shore to summit with mine shafts, and those who dig them seldom go home.

The [[Locations/Azure Plains Coalition (APC)/index|coalition]] puts no one to death, for life is too precious to waste. Instead, its condemned are sold. Captured pirates are bought for the prize money owed to the crews who took them, and convicts for the cost of their passage, split between the buyer and the island that sentenced them. Even the [[Locations/Kingdom of Albryn/index|Kingdom]], which keeps no great prisons of its own, sells its convicts and the pirates it takes on the [[Locations/The Black Tide/index|Black Tide]] border to the coalition. All of them are carried by the black ships, which sail from island jail to island jail gathering the condemned, and aboard every black ship rides a deputy of [[Locations/Azure Plains Coalition (APC)/Sparte Torro/index|Sparte Torro]], who walks the hold at each port and buys whichever prisoners its fields can use. These are put ashore at the next harbor to wait for a ship north. The rest stay chained below, and the black ship carries them on to Isla Embra. Most are sent there for life. Those with a sentence to serve are freed on the day it ends, if they can walk into town and present themselves at the fortress gate, and few ever do. The only other way out is a pardon from the coalition council, which is granted rarely and never cheaply.

The island's galleries are the richest source of tin and lead in the coalition, and they yield iron, copper and zinc besides. Once in a long while a convict breaks open a pocket of rubies or sapphires deep in the rock; the commissary pays a small fortune in tallies for each stone, and the gems are among the rarest in the coalition. There are no smelters here. Every scrap of ore is carried to the harbor on the convicts' own backs and loaded aboard the same black ships that brought them, to be sold raw across the coalition, and that ore is the council's greatest source of wealth, paying for the coalition army and the customs cutters alike. The coming and going of the black ships is the only thing on Isla Embra that resembles a calendar. At night the hillsides glitter with the lamps of convicts going down the shafts, and they call the island the Ember.

The island's only town clings to the harbor beneath the fortress walls, and there is little enough of it: a guard barracks, a quay, and the commissary, and these are the only places the guards ever set foot. The prisoners have no dormitories. Most live in holes dug into the earth around the town, a great warren of burrows and tunnels that grows a little deeper every year. Nothing on Isla Embra is against the law. There is no quota and no overseer, and no one is made to dig. They do not need to be. Every load of ore delivered earns a crude stamped iron tally from the commissary, worthless anywhere else in the world and impossible to copy without a forge, and those tallies are the island's only money. The commissary window sells anything a prisoner can afford except a weapon: food, clean water, liquor and tobacco. Those who dig, eat. Those who do not, starve. Most can afford only gruel. The strongest gangs hold the richest mine heads, and their bosses decide who digs and who eats there. Those with a knack for it have turned the warren into a market of its own: diggers who carve out new homes for a price, brothels, gambling dens and tattooists, loan sharks who advance tallies to the hungry at ruinous rates, fixers who arrange any service a prisoner cannot arrange alone for a cut, and a dozen other trades for anyone with tallies to spend on a vice or a better hole to sleep in. The island's own water is fouled by the mines, nothing grows on the rock, and no tree has ever stood there, so no convict has ever built a raft. A revolt would starve within weeks, even before the mortars answered it.

Above the harbor looms a well-stocked fortress, the base of the southern coalition's customs cutters, and offshore the mortar ships ride at anchor with their guns trained on the town and the coast. They are seldom fired. They do not need to be. They are large, well-armed ships besides, and they serve as the reserve for the whole southeast, ready to sail wherever warships are needed in a hurry, and as a quiet reminder to the Kingdom across the border that the coalition is not undefended. The fortress holds one mercy: women late in their pregnancy are taken inside its walls to give birth, and the children are carried away to be raised elsewhere, by the temples of [[Morvaine]], [[Apiot]] and the other gods, and by any island that will take them in. The mothers are returned to the island. Few would choose to raise a child on Isla Embra, and fewer still would risk a revolt while the women in the fortress are hostages to it.

The only free soul who walks among the prisoners is the gravesman, who lives alone in a small stone chapel by a cove on the far side of the island. From there he sends the dead out on their funeral boats, for even here, Morvaine remembers.

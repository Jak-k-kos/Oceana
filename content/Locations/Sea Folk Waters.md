# DM Notes
- The sea folk of the [[Locations/The Widow's Wrath/index|Widow's Wrath]] are runaways from the Dominion and their descendants. The Wrath shelters them from the empire they fled, and with no land dwellers living in it, they never have to test their luck with humanoids either. See [[Siren]] for the truth of the sirens.
- The Dominion's forges are smithies built over the deep's boiling vents, where metal can be softened without fire. The work is slow, scarce and deadly, which is why metal is so precious below.
- Who, or what, sits upon the Coral Throne is the DM's call.
- Kuo-toa devotion, the sea elves' place in the Dominion's order and the Dominion's history are as true as the tales say.
- False: [[Caruilla]] sits the Coral Throne.
- False: the [[Locations/The Black Tide/index|Black Tide]] pays the Dominion. The Black Tide is raided as often as anyone; no coalition or Kingdom chart shows its islands, so the surface simply never sees it.
- False: the Dominion seeks the drowned door to Vhaldrun. It has no wish to loose the fiends again.

## The Selkies
The selkies are not of the Dominion at all, and they are not truly creatures of the deep. Like the seals they run with, they can only hold their breath. They live between the tides, on lonely rocks and remote beaches among the seal colonies, belonging wholly to neither the land nor the sea. Even the gods cannot agree whose they are, for [[Ruthala]] and [[Caruilla]] have squabbled over the seals for ages. The rumor that they spy for the Dominion is false. A few have defected to the islands all the same.

# Sea Folk Waters
Beyond every shore and beneath every hull lies the Thalassine Dominion. Sailors call it simply the Dominion. It is the greatest power in the world, and no one who lives above the waves has ever seen its heart.

# The Coral Throne
The Dominion is ruled from what the surface calls the Coral Throne, and it is the closest thing to a name its ruler has ever had. Who sits upon it, how the Dominion is governed and where the Throne itself lies, no land dweller knows. Many sailors believe it is [[Caruilla]] herself who sits upon it, ruling her children in person, though no one has ever proven it.

Few have ever gone looking. The rare warriors who dove deep enough to glimpse the Dominion's waters came back with tales of horrors they could barely put into words, and most did not come back at all. Only one city of the Dominion has ever been seen by surface eyes: the one that grew up around the cauldron of Magnus, where [[The Tidebreakers]] fought their way in and out again. Even they speak little of what they saw there.

# The Deep Provides
For all the surface can tell, the Dominion needs nothing from it. Sailors speak of drowned mines worked by sea folk, and the few who have glimpsed the kelp forests unseen swear they are farmed like fields. What is known for certain is that the sea folk take every scrap of metal they can carry from every ship and shore they raid. Some say it rusts away to nothing in the deep and they can never have enough; no one truly knows.

Scholars say it was not always so rich. The deep sea is a barren place, and they believe the sea folk were few before the flood. Then the flood drowned the lowlands, and drowned lowlands become shallow seas, the richest waters in the world. As [[Apiot]] turned her hand to the gardens of the sea, what had once been bare sand became meadows of sea grass and forests of kelp, and the sea folk, it seems, have multiplied beyond counting ever since.

# The Last Shallows
Yet the shallows the Dominion grew rich upon are mostly not its own anymore. Since [[The Coming of Powder]], nearly every reef and shoal worth having has been taken by the land and is held by cannon and gun. A few still lie within the Dominion's waters, but most of the world's reefs are now the last strongholds of the surface, and the sea folk have never forgiven it.

# The Peoples of the Deep
The Dominion is not one people but many, all of them children of [[Caruilla]]. What the surface knows of them, it has learned the hard way.

**Kuo-toa.** The Dominion's great masses. Sailors say there are more kuo-toa than all the other sea folk together, and that there's barely a thought among the lot of them. They are the spears that fill every raid, the grunts of the deep, and what they lack in wit they make up in fervor. They fight to the last, and they die screaming the name of [[Caruilla]].

**Sahuagin.** Second only to the kuo-toa in number, and first in savagery. The sahuagin are the Dominion's raiders, shark-toothed and shark-riding, and most of the surface's darkest stories begin with them.

**Merfolk.** Of the merfolk, the surface knows little: not their numbers, nor what they do in the Dominion. What is known for certain is that they rarely ride with the raiding parties, and those that do seem to lack the hatred of their kin, not that any sailor would bet his life on it. The only glimpses ever caught of them beyond the strongholds of the land are of merfolk at work in the kelp forests and the deep meadows, with locathah at their sides. And of the very few sea folk who have ever defected to the surface, most have been merfolk.

**Locathah.** Small, slight fish-folk, glimpsed now and then working alongside the merfolk in the kelp forests. No locathah has ever been seen in a raiding party, not once, and so few have been seen at all that there are sailors who swear they don't exist, and that the glimpses are only merfolk seen through murky water.

**Sea elves.** Few in number and many in talent. Sea elves are the silent watchers of the shipping lanes, scouts and hunters who see a ship long before it sees them. Sailors suspect they are a great deal more besides, and that whatever order the Dominion keeps beneath the waves, sea elves are keeping much of it.

**Tritons.** As clever as the merfolk and as brutal as the sahuagin. Whenever a war-host has had a leader, it has nearly always been a triton, and when the Dominion falls upon a ship, it is a triton below the hull that sailors learn to dread. That is why the betrayal of Nerith of the Deep, a triton who led the Tidebreakers to the cauldron, is said to have cut so deep below.

**Merrow.** Larger, stronger and more savage than even the sahuagin, merrow are the rage of the deep made manifest. The Dominion throws them first into any fight, and it is said even the other sea folk give them a wide berth.

**Cecaelias.** Half octopus and half man or woman, cunning, cruel and slow to forgive. Every crew dreads a raid with cecaelias in it, for they are always seen in the same way at the end: dragging armfuls of screaming sailors down beneath the waves, more than any other sea folk could ever carry.

**Sirens.** Beautiful, pitiless and always women, sirens sing sailors over the rail and down into the dark. Much is told of them in every port. See [[Siren]].

**Selkies.** Seal-folk who can shed their skins and walk the land in mortal shape. Of all the sea folk, only the selkies can pass unnoticed among the islands, and the islands whisper about them constantly. Some say they are the Dominion's spies, walking the docks in borrowed skins. Others tell of sailors who come home from a far voyage with a lovely, silent new wife or husband who walks down to the shore each evening, and of a sealskin locked away somewhere it can never be found. None of it makes them welcome. A selkie discovered on an island is almost never allowed to leave it alive.

**Sea hags.** Ancient, hideous and wise in the old, cruel ways. Sea hags are the priests of [[Caruilla]], or so sailors say, for they come with the raiding parties shrieking of their foul mistress of the sea. Of all the raids a crew can face, few are dreaded more than a sea hag at the head of a flock of kuo-toa, the fish-folk driven forward by her ravings in a fever no cannon can break.

# An Old Hatred
There was never peace between the land and the sea. For as long as anyone has kept records, the sea folk raided the shores, and the land dwellers slaughtered them on sight. It was a war without end, and one neither side could ever truly win. The sea folk could not push far inland, and the land dwellers could not follow them into the deep. Scholars say the sea folk were few in those days, for the deep could feed only so many and the rich shallows were held by the peoples of the land. Any attempt by either side to truly break the other would only have brought the great archmagi of both into the fight.

Nor did the land only kill. Old tales tell of sailors who stole a selkie's skin to force her to wed them, and of fishermen who hauled up a mermaid, kept her for her beauty and slaughtered the rest of her kind. The sea folk remember every one.

The flood changed who was winning. With every drowned mile, the battlefield came to the sea folk's door, and they were able to raid the shrinking shores with little the land could do in answer. The Dominion fought on three sides against [[The Eviction|the Legions of Vhaldrun]] and the land dwellers alike, and it broke the fiends first. Its peoples guarded the cauldron of Magnus for more than three thousand years. The [[Arcanus Collapse]] stripped their magic as surely as the surface's, and [[The Coming of Powder]] drove them back for the first time in living memory. They have never forgotten it.

# What the Deep Takes
Those the sea folk carry off are never seen again, and the surface has no doubt why. The Dominion has no use for prisoners. It wants metal, and it wants flesh. Sailors tell of captives dragged down to pockets of air in the deep, kept alive in the dark so that the Dominion's young may feed on them while they still breathe. No one has ever come back to say whether it is true. It is why sailors say the lucky ones are those who die on the deck.

# No Trade and No Word
There is no trade with the Dominion, no embassy and no treaty, and there never has been. Many of its peoples know the common tongue, but they use it only to lure and to threaten. A rare few sea folk have defected and live among the islands, most of them merfolk, and not one of them will ever speak of home. Those who turn against the Dominion are called traitors below, it is said, as Nerith of the Deep is now.

# The Night of the Dance
For all their hatred, the sea folk keep at least one law of the land. On [[The Siblings' Dance]], when all three moons shine full, the raids cease, and sailors have learned that the Dominion honors the moons' truce as surely as the islands do. Not every one of its peoples seems to care. Crews who have seen kuo-toa and sea hags in the water on the Dance say they strain at the truce like dogs on a chain, and only their commanders hold them back.

# The Restless Deep
Since [[The Breaking of the Cauldron]], the Dominion has grown restless. Raids have grown bolder and more frequent along the [[Locations/Azure Plains Coalition (APC)/index|coalition's]] northern shores, across the waters of the [[Locations/Kingdom of Albryn/index|Kingdom]] and in the scattered havens of [[Locations/Smuggler's Reach/index|Smuggler's Reach]]. No harbor sleeps easy now.

Yet in the taverns of the coalition and the Kingdom, a bitter tale is passed from table to table. It is said that the [[Locations/The Black Tide/index|Black Tide]] has struck a bargain with the deep, paying the Dominion in metal and in captives to turn its hunger north, upon the coalition and the Kingdom, and leave the pirates of the south in peace. Who first heard it, no one can say, but every fresh raid on a coalition shore makes it a little easier to believe.

Darker still are the whispers that the Dominion is searching for the drowned door to [[The Drowning of Vhaldrun|Vhaldrun]]. Somewhere in the black deep, the tale goes, the sea folk are clearing away the ruin of ages, meaning to open the door at last and loose whatever still waits in the dry heights upon the surface world. If the Dominion cannot drown the land, the story warns, it will let something else devour it.

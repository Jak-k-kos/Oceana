# DM Notes
- Leader: None
- Food: Wild figs, oranges, lemons and grapes from the old terraces; wild goats on the slopes; seabird eggs on the northern arm; fresh fish from the lagoons
- Trade: None
- extra: Everything on [[Locations/The Widow's Wrath/Rayskaya/index|Rayskaya]] beyond the peak, [[Locations/The Widow's Wrath/Rayskaya/The Cove|the cove]] and [[Locations/The Widow's Wrath/Rayskaya/The Drowned City|the drowned city]].
  - What it was: The highest reaches of the old city, the part the sea never claimed. The terraces were its orchards and vineyards, and the ruined houses among them belonged to the wealthy, who built high on the slopes to look down over the city.
  - The western spine: A long, high ridge of jungle and terrace running north to south. Two lagoons lie tucked into it, both fed by springs from the heights. Their water is fresh and clear.
  - The northern arm: A long, thin ridge of rock and scrub reaching east across the water. Seabirds nest along it by the thousands. At its very tip stands the stump of an old watchtower that once kept watch over the mouth of the cove.
  - Wildlife: Wild goats, descended from the herds the city left behind. Seabirds, parrots and lizards. No predators larger than a wild cat. The island is safe.
  - Hooks (use or ignore):
    - Not everything in the ruined villas has rotted away.
    - An overgrown shrine on the western spine, to a god the party has never heard of. Which god is the DM's call.

# The Terraces
Beyond the peak and [[Locations/The Widow's Wrath/Rayskaya/The Cove|the cove]], the rest of [[Locations/The Widow's Wrath/Rayskaya/index|Rayskaya]] belongs to the green.

Jungle tumbles down every slope, a riot of broad leaves, hanging vines and flowers in colors that have no names. Beneath it, the bones of an older order still show through: long stone terraces stepping down the hillsides like the seats of some great amphitheater, their walls slumped and moss-furred, their soil gone wild. Fig trees lean heavy with fruit. Oranges and lemons glow among the leaves like little lanterns. Grapevines, long since forgotten by any vintner, have climbed the terrace walls and spilled down them in tangled green cascades. All of it is free for the taking, and no one has taken it in a very long time.

Here and there among the trees stand the shells of fine old houses, their roofs fallen in and their courtyards turned to gardens of fern. Wild goats pick their way along the ruined walls, and parrots scold from the rafters.

Along the western spine, two lagoons lie cupped among the hills, so clear and still that they hold a perfect mirror of the sky. To the north, a long arm of bare rock reaches out across the sea, loud with the cries of a thousand nesting seabirds, and at its very end the stump of an old tower stands watch over a mouth of water no ship has sailed through in ages.

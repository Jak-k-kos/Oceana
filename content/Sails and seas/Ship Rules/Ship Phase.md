After the [[Character Phase]] ends, ships take their turns, highest initiative first. Ships use their fixed initiative score plus any bonus from the Command Crew Action. On its turn, a ship can use all the movement and actions its crew earned in the Character Phase.

# Movement
Most ships have no base speed. They gain movement each round from Sail Crew Actions.
- Like creatures, ships can split up their movement and act in between moves.
- A ship doesn't have to use all its movement, but anything unused is lost at the end of the round.
- Movement is measured from the **bow**: distance is how far the bow travels.
- Ships can't take the Dash action.
- Facing matters. Ships can't move backward; reversing takes a full 180 degree turn. Most weapons also depend on facing to aim.
- **Turning:** Unless it's Agile, a ship can't turn on the spot. It turns while moving, up to 45 degrees for each Speed Unit of movement spent. (A ship with a 10 ft. Speed Unit needs at least 40 ft. of movement to make a 180 degree turn.)

# Attacking
A ship gets one attack for each Operate Crew Action taken on its weapons during the Character Phase.
- The creature who operated the weapon rolls the attack, using their own ability modifiers and proficiencies. If they didn't pick a target earlier, they pick one now.
- Attacks can be made at any point on the ship's turn, including between moves.
- Unused attacks are lost at the end of the round.
- If the crew member who operated a weapon is incapacitated before it fires, that attack is lost.
- Ship weapons can also target creatures aboard another ship, as long as they're visible and in range. Medium and larger ships usually have rails and walls on their top decks, giving everyone aboard at least half cover.

# Grappling
Some ship weapons can grapple another ship (see each weapon for how).
- A ship grappled by a ship of a larger size class gains no movement from Sail Crew Actions and can't benefit from any bonus to its speed.
- **Escaping:** The grapple ends when its source is destroyed (like cutting the grappling hook loose) or when an effect moves the grappled ship out of the grappling weapon's reach, or the other way around.
- **Towing:** A ship can bring a ship it has grappled along when it moves. The grappled ship stays the same distance from the grapple's point of origin the whole time. Each foot moved costs an extra foot of speed, unless the grappled ship is two sizes smaller than the grappler.

# Ramming
A last resort, or a way to get up close.
- **Requirements:** The ship must be adjacent to the target and have enough movement left to enter its space.
- **Attack:** The ship makes an attack roll at +5 against the target. If someone took the Helm Crew Action this turn, they can add their Water Vehicles proficiency.
- **Hit:** The rammed ship takes damage equal to the rammer's **maximum** Hull Points. The rammer takes half the rammed ship's maximum Hull Points.
- **Miss:** Reversed. The rammed ship takes half the rammer's maximum Hull Points, and the rammer takes the rammed ship's full maximum Hull Points.
- **Knocked prone:** If either ship takes more than half its own maximum Hull Points from the ram, everyone aboard that ship makes a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw or falls prone.
- **Stuck together:** Afterward, the two ships are tangled in wreckage and grappling each other until one of them is destroyed.
- A ship can only ram once per turn.

# Reactions
Ships can ready actions using the normal Player's Handbook rules: on its turn, a ship declares the readied action and its trigger. This is handy for waiting until an enemy sails into range before firing.
- Unlike a creature, a ship can ready as many actions as it has available, but must declare the order it will take them in.
- A readied action not used by the end of the round is lost.
- Ships can't make opportunity attacks.

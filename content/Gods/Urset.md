The new god of magic. Patron of mages and the greedy.

## Titles

- The Hoarder
- The Usurper
- The Crippled God
- The Miser of the Lower Halls
- Mortimer (used only as an insult, and never where he might hear it)
## Pre-Divinity  
Urset was born Mortimer Greenbe in 107 PAC in Veygard, the last great nation of the old world. Veygard had stood against the flood for four hundred years by the time he was born, raising walls and wards against the tidal waves, taking in refugees by the thousand and meeting the sea folk on its shores. Mortimer was the eighth prince of the Veygard royal family. Generations of inbreeding among the nobility, and his mother's heavy drug and alcohol use while she carried him, left him horribly deformed. Barely able to walk or complete simple tasks, and shunned by his family and the court, he spent his early life in a hidden wing of the castle under the constant care of servants.

Confined to his rooms, he turned to books as his only escape, and to magic above all else. He spent nearly every waking hour studying arcane texts, and by the age of fifteen he had become self-sufficient, using magic to meet the needs his body could not. His talent was unparalleled: he became an archmage at twenty-three, an unprecedented achievement even in the age before the [[Arcanus Collapse]]. He soon made himself indispensable to the kingdom, and at thirty-six he was named Head Magos of Veygard.

As his power grew, so did his greed. When Mortimer was fifty-four, his father died and the throne passed to his eldest brother. Mortimer attempted a coup to seize it for himself, and failed. His brother could not afford to kill him, for Veygard's strength rested too heavily on his magic, but neither could he ever trust him again. Mortimer was stripped of his title and sealed away in a warded tower, where he was kept working for the crown as a prisoner for the rest of his mortal life.

He spent more than fifty years in that tower. The crown kept him supplied with the tomes and artifacts his work required, never noticing how many of them failed to come back out. He lived far beyond a mortal span, sustained, it is said, by the very relics he hoarded. In 1 PAC, following the death of [[Arkaius]], Mortimer saw his opportunity. Through magics no one has been able to name, he seized the fading pieces of [[Arkaius]]'s soul from within his cell and bound them to his own, making himself the new god of magic.

Yet not all of it became his. A sliver of [[Arkaius]] slipped from his grasp and passed into the three moons, [[Ellehwin]], [[Ostin]], and [[Winstra]], the children of [[Arkaius]] and [[Welestia]].

## Divinity  
His ascension reshaped his body, freeing him at last from the deformities that had chained him since birth. Embracing his newfound power, he took the name Urset and rose to the Halls of Divinity to claim his predecessor's place.

His first act as a god was to draw the magic of the world into himself, as much as he could hold, for his greed had no end. Through the rest of that year, the rivers of magic ran dry. Cut off from their source, the magic items and mages that had once been plentiful withered and died away. This was the [[Arcanus Collapse]], and the world has counted its years from it ever since. Without its mages, Veygard's wards went dark, and in [[The Fall of Veygard|the year 41]] the kingdom that had imprisoned him was swallowed by the sea.

Soon after, Urset attempted to force the newly widowed [[Welestia]] to become his bride, believing her his by right as the god of magic. Enraged by his impudence, [[Welestia]] unleashed her wrath upon him. Even with his divine constitution, he was powerless against the full strength she had held back through all the ages of her marriage. The attack left his body burned and crippled. It was only the first storm of a fury that has never ended.

Crippled and shamed, Urset was driven down into the lower Halls of Divinity. He remains there to this day, hoarding the magic he refuses to allow the world to wield. Many believe he waits there still, plotting a divine coup against the gods who refused to accept him.

## The Cruel Heart  
Urset is as cruel as he is greedy. The drowning of the world never troubled him. Many believe it delighted him. After the Weaver fell, he was one of only two gods who might have reached the cauldron, and he never lifted a finger. When Veygard fell, it is said he watched every moment of it, and laughed. Some even whisper that it is his stolen magic that kept the cauldron pouring all those years, though no one has ever proven it.

## Worship  
Urset is the patron of mages and of the greedy: those who want more than they have, and will pay any price to get it.

Every mage draws their power from Urset, whether they worship him or not. He gives it out grudgingly, a trickle at a time, and always at a cost. Mages bargain for their power with offerings, service, or favors he may not call in for years.

Not every caster has struck a bargain with him, however. A rare few rangers, bards, artificers, and druids draw their magic from elsewhere. Some have found ways to steal slivers of his hoard, while others are granted power by one of the three moons, whose fragment of [[Arkaius]] lies beyond his reach. The dragons, too, steal from him freely, aided by [[Koruk]] himself, and the rare sorcerer with dragon's blood in their veins draws on that same stolen magic. Urset forgives neither the thief nor the moon-blessed. Darker rumors speak of those who cast with their own blood, paying for their magic in pain rather than in Urset's favor, though none have ever been proven to exist.

Few cities will raise a temple to the shamed god, so his worship is a private affair: a locked room, a hidden shrine, a prayer muttered over a counting table. His followers are mages, moneylenders, ambitious nobles, and anyone scheming to take what is not theirs. Offerings must be something of real value to the giver, such as gold, rare relics, or secrets. It is said he answers most readily those who offer what they can least afford to lose.

### Champion  
Sailors whisper that Urset's champion is the [[Dread Pirate King Vask]], and that the god who gives nothing freely has poured his power into a single mortal. If the rumors are true, the god who failed to take a throne by force may have found someone to take one for him.

## Relationships

- **The Pantheon**: Urset despises nearly all the gods, for none of them would accept him as one of their own.
- **[[Welestia]]**: She burned and crippled him, and he hates her for it above all others. Yet he wants her still. Making her his would be both his revenge and his desire, and he has never stopped scheming toward it.
- **[[Ellehwin]], [[Ostin]] & [[Winstra]]**: The moons hold the pieces of [[Arkaius]] that slipped from his grasp, and they hand out the power of his domain to whomever they please. To Urset, every moon-blessed caster is a theft.
- **[[Ostin]]**: Of the three, he hates [[Ostin]] most. [[Ostin]]'s progress offers mortals a path to power that needs no magic at all, and a world with no need for Urset.
- **[[Koruk]]**: The dragon god who helps his children rob Urset's hoard, and who looks upon him as a pale imitation of a dragon's might. Urset counts him among his greatest enemies.
- **[[Tarrakus]]**: The closest thing Urset has to an ally, and a puzzling choice of friend for a god as level-headed and calculating as [[Tarrakus]].

God of earth. Patron of miners and the land-born races.

## Titles

- The Unmoving
- The Patient Stone
- Holder of the Islands
- The Deep Root
- Father of Mountains

## The Bones of the World  
Long ago, Roktra raised the bones of the world. He piled the mountains high and carved the valleys deep, laid down the bedrock beneath every field and the stone beneath every sea, and in the shaping of it all, he became the earth itself: vast and patient and slow to change.

He is a quiet god, and a stubborn one, and he is endlessly steady. When the storms of the heavens rage and the other gods are tossed about by grief and fury and ambition, Roktra does not move. He is the rock in the storm, the one thing that can always be counted upon to stand where it stood the day before. He speaks seldom, moves slowly and gives his word rarely, but once it is given, it holds like bedrock. Those who mistake his stillness for weakness have never seen a mountain fall.

## The Holder of the Islands  
When the waters began to rise, Roktra could not turn them back. The sea belonged to [[Aquilla]], and by the First Law it lay beyond his reach. But the land was his, and he would not let it go without a fight.

Through all the long centuries of [[Aquilla's Wrath]], Roktra held the line. He heaved great shelves of stone up from below, lifted the mountains ever higher and braced the land against the climbing tide, refusing to let it slip beneath the waves. He could not save it all. The world lost far more than it kept, and some islands would have stood without him. But many would not. A great many of the islands where mortals live today rise above the sea only because Roktra refused, century after century, to let them sink.

## Fire From the Deep  
Even now, Roktra has not surrendered a single inch more than he must. Every so often, far out at sea, the water begins to boil. The sky darkens with ash, the waves glow red from below, and out of fire and smoke a new island rises from the deep. These are Roktra's gifts, new dry land torn back from the sea, and among the land-born, the birth of a new island is a holy thing, spoken of for generations.

## A Quiet Strength  
Many believe the flood must have weakened Roktra, that every drowned mile of land was a mile of his domain lost to [[Aquilla]]. They are mistaken. The land beneath the sea is land still, and the stone at the bottom of the deepest ocean is his as surely as any mountaintop. Dry land is what he loves best, and what he fights for, but it was never the whole of his power.

The truth is that there is far more earth in the world than there is water upon it, and Roktra may well be one of the mightiest of all the gods. He simply has never felt the need to show it.

## The Land-Born  
The land-born races are Roktra's own children in all but blood: the peoples who have always made their homes in stone and soil, and who suffered most when the land drowned. The dwarves above all lost their ancient mountain holds and deep mines to the flood, and many of their clans are now scattered across the islands, digging what little rock remains above the waves.

In an island world, stone and ore have become precious things. Metal is scarce, miners are honored, and the boldest of them now dig beneath the sea itself, sinking shafts and lowering diving bells into the drowned mines of old. It is dangerous work in dark water, and the sea folk rarely welcome it.

### Temples  
Roktra's churches are steady things. Each one is built first and built to last, of thick stone set deep into the bedrock, and on many islands his church is the oldest building standing. Some survived the whole of the flood when everything around them was lost.

When [[Welestia|Welestia's]] storms come howling in from the sea, the whole town takes shelter within Roktra's church. Its walls have weathered more hurricanes than anyone can count, and they have never yet fallen. It is the rock in the storm, just as its god is.

With dry land so precious, Roktra's priests are also its keepers. They hold the records of who owns each plot of land, survey its borders and help to settle disputes over it, so that no one's ground is ever lost to a lie. Many of his priests are stonemasons and surveyors by trade.

## Worship  
Roktra is the patron of miners and of the land-born, and of all who build to last.

- **A stone from home.** Many land-born families carry a small stone from their drowned ancestral lands, passed down from parent to child, so that they will never forget the ground they came from.
- **The cornerstone.** The foundation stone of every new building is blessed in Roktra's name, so that what is built upon it will stand.
- **The first ore.** The first ore drawn from any new mine is offered to Roktra.
- **Down and up.** Miners pray to him before they descend into the dark, and give him thanks when they climb back into the light.

### Champion  
Roktra's champion is hidden, and few have ever thought to wonder about them. Like their god, they are likely as quiet and as steady as stone.

## Relationships

- **[[Aquilla]]**: Every mile of [[Aquilla|Aquilla's]] sea was once his dry land. Theirs is among the bitterest rivalries in the pantheon, though Roktra's anger is slow and silent, like stone settling, and all the more lasting for it.
- **[[Apiot]]**: He holds up the land, and she makes it bloom. Theirs is a quiet and steady alliance, and with so little dry land left in the world, they guard what remains together.
- **[[Ostin]]**: [[Ostin|Ostin's]] diving bells and engines reach the drowned mines that Roktra still watches over. The patient god and the restless one get along far better than anyone expected.
- **[[Arkaius]]**: Roktra stood beside [[Arkaius]] through all the ages of his rule, never wavering, and he mourns him still in his own silent way.
- **[[Ellehwin]]**: He has given [[Ellehwin|Ellehwin's]] rule the same unshaken loyalty he gave her father. When the halls of the gods are in turmoil, she knows that Roktra, at least, will not move.
- **[[Ruthala]]**: When the flood stripped the wild from her, Roktra granted her a share in the land-born peoples he shaped. Man, he reminded the heavens, is but a beast too.

God of music. Patron of bards, musicians and lovers.

## Titles

- The Great Muse
- The Beloved
- Lord of Song
- The First Note
- The Wandering Minstrel

## The Song of All Things  
Lyandris shaped the music of the world, and in doing so took on its aspect. He gave voice to the wind in the rigging and the rain upon the roof, to the lullaby and the battle hymn, to the sailor's shanty and the mourner's dirge. Every living thing has its own song, from the birds of the islands to the great whales singing in the deep, and every one of them is a little piece of his.

He is a carefree and joyful spirit, though never a loud one. His is a quiet happiness, the kind found in a half-hummed tune on a summer evening, and it is said that no one has ever sat in his company and come away sadder than they arrived.

## The Beloved  
By the reckoning of the heavens, Lyandris is a lower god, and he always has been. Yet no one has ever treated him as one. Over the ages, without any ruling made or any vote cast, the upper gods simply began to make room for him at their table, and he has sat among them ever since. No god has ever objected, for no god has ever wished to. He is perhaps the only god with no enemies but one, beloved by mortals and gods alike.

[[Caruilla]], who has begged for a place among the upper gods for centuries, never fails to bring him up when she complains about it. He was given the seat without the title, and she wants both. She bears him no ill will, though. It is very hard to be angry with Lyandris.

## The Muse Among Mortals  
Every now and then, Lyandris slips down into the mortal world, and somewhere a tavern finds itself host to the finest musician it has ever heard. He plays through the night for anyone who will listen, and by morning he is gone. Tales of these concerts are told across every island, and half the bards in the world claim to have played alongside him at least once. Yet no one can ever be sure. Was it the god himself, or his champion, or simply a mortal bard blessed with a rare and wondrous gift? Those who were there will swear to any of the three, and none can prove it.

Lyandris has loved mortals, though never lightly. He has never taken a lover he did not truly love, and each time they die, it breaks him. Yet he finds beauty even in that grief, for he has watched his dearest friend, [[Morvaine]], shepherd their souls gently onward into their next lives. Whatever children those loves have given him, he has never once left unwatched, and for that, [[Apiot]] holds him in the highest regard.

## The Bards' Secret  
When [[Urset]] drank the magic of the world dry, it was Lyandris who found the crack in his hoard. The right song, sung with enough heart, can coax a thread of magic loose from [[Urset]]'s grasp, and Lyandris taught the first bards how. [[Urset]] rages at the theft, but he can do nothing about it. To stop it, he would have to silence music itself, and that would set every god in the heavens against him.

Even so, the gift is a rare one. Most who call themselves bards are simply minstrels: singers, players and storytellers whose only magic is the kind any good song carries. Only a rare few ever learn to sing a thread of true magic loose, and fewer still learn to do it well.

### Temples  
Lyandris's temples are less churches than celebration halls: bright, open places built for weddings, feasts, festivals and performances. Most are more venue than sanctuary, and on any given night, one is far more likely to find dancing inside than prayer.

His priests are all musicians, and they officiate the weddings held in his halls. Every wedding has music, and so nearly every wedding in the islands has a priest of Lyandris at its heart, playing the couple down the aisle and singing them into their new life together.

## Worship  
Every note is a prayer to Lyandris, and most songs in the world are sung in his name. Tavern bards dedicate their first song of the night to him, and often many more after it. On every ship, the shanties that keep a crew hauling together are small prayers of their own, and sailors say a crew that sings together comes home together.

Yet for most, worship of Lyandris is not about asking for anything. It is a thanksgiving for the music he brought into the world, an honoring rather than a plea. Lovers are the exception. They alone truly pray to him, for a song to win a heart, or for one to keep it.

Lyandris asks for nothing in return but that his followers sing, play and create without holding back. To him, a song played timidly is the only real insult.

### Champion  
If Lyandris has a champion at all, they are hidden, perhaps some famous bard whose songs are known from one end of the world to the other. Whoever they are, they rarely use their power in any way that could be noticed. Lyandris has never had much interest in the matter of champions. He would rather simply play.

## Relationships

- **[[Morvaine]]**: His dearest friend. Where the other gods see only the cold mystery of death and fate, Lyandris sees the person beneath, and he loves her as a brother loves a sister. [[Morvaine]], for her part, loves his music and his heart. Some say she smiles only for him.
- **[[Fennara]]**: The closest thing either of them has to family. Lyandris is the one god [[Fennara]] would never bet against.
- **[[Flaris]]**: The fire and the song, two of the three seats at every good tavern table. No evening at [[Flaris]]'s hall is complete without him.
- **[[Apiot]]**: She respects him as few others, for he has never once left a child of his unwatched.
- **[[Caruilla]]**: He sits at the table she longs for, and she never lets anyone forget it, though she has never managed to stay cross with him.
- **[[Urset]]**: The only god who has no love for him. Lyandris taught the bards to steal from his hoard, and [[Urset]] can do nothing about it.

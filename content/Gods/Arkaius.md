The dead god of magic. Once head of the pantheon.

## Titles

- The Weaver
- The Lawgiver
- First Among Gods
- Father of Moons
- The Silent God (used by his mourning cults)

## The Threads of the World  
Arkaius spun the magic of the world, the shimmering threads that run unseen through all things, and in the spinning he became its keeper. Magic stirs in the flame and the wave, in the stone and the sky, in the first breath of life and the last, and so his domain reached into the domain of every other god. No other god could claim such a thing, and for this, as much as for his wisdom, the gods crowned him first among them.

He was good, and he was mighty, and for untold ages he ruled the halls of the gods with a gentle and steady hand. Gods and mortals alike loved him. In his day, magic ran through the world like rivers through a valley. Mages were as common as merchants, and wonders were sold in the marketplace.

## The First Law  
It was Arkaius who spoke the law that binds the gods still: that no god may lay their hand upon the world beyond their own domain. He had seen what unbridled gods could do to the mortal world, and he swore it would never be their plaything. Though his own domain touched all things, he bowed to the law as humbly as the least of them. The law has long outlived its maker, and today it is kept by his eldest daughter, [[Ellehwin]].

## The Storm and the Eye  
Where the other gods took mortal lovers and scattered demigod children across the world, Arkaius gave his heart once, and to one of his own. He wed [[Welestia]], the storm, and theirs remains the only marriage the heavens have ever known. From their union came three children, the moons [[Ellehwin]], [[Ostin]] and [[Winstra]], the only gods ever born of gods.

Welestia was never gentle by nature. The hurricanes are older than his death, and many whispered that she was as mad as the skies she ruled. Arkaius never tried to tame her. He was the eye of her storm, the stillness at its heart, and for love of him she held her fury in check. In those days she sent soft rains to the fields and fair winds to the sails. Since he fell, there has been nothing left to hold her.

## The Fall of the Weaver  
In 1 PAC, Arkaius died. On that same day his champion fell as well, a mortal whose name the ages have since swallowed, and no one knows which of the two fell first. How a god may die at all is a mystery that has never been answered, and the hand that struck him down, if hand it was, has never been found.

As his soul unraveled, a mortal mage named Mortimer Greenbe gathered up its fraying threads and bound them to himself, and rose as the god [[Urset]]. Yet a few threads slipped through his grasping fingers and drifted up to the three moons, so that a little of their father lives on in each of them. Within the year came the Arcanus Collapse, as Urset drank the magic of the world dry.

The throne of the gods passed to [[Ellehwin]], for of all the gods she was the only one every other would suffer to sit there.

### Whispers of the Guilty  
The rumors of his murder have never died, though not one has ever been proven.

- **[[Welestia]]** bears the most blame. Her temper was legend, and her rage after his death was so terrible that many swear no grief alone could have birthed it.
- **[[Urset]]** gained the most. Some wonder how a mortal mage, locked away in his tower, stood ready at the very moment a god breathed his last.
- **[[Morvaine]]** is death itself, and some say that no one else could have done it.
- **[[Koruk]]** was, in those days, as savage and cruel as the chromatic dragons he ruled, and he and Arkaius had clashed more than once. Only in the flood times did the dragon god grow calm, and some wonder whether it was guilt that quieted him.
- **[[Tarrakus]]** is named only by the most cynical. The war god is patient and calculating, and the heavens have never been the same since.
- **Arkaius himself.** A rare few dare to wonder whether the wisest of the gods chose his own ending, and whether he saw something on the horizon that made death the kinder road. No one has ever given a reason why he would.

## The Faithful Few  
Arkaius is all but forgotten in prayer. Here and there, small mourning cults still light candles in his name and whisper to a god who can no longer hear them. They are few and they are frail, and no blessing has ever answered them, for the dead cannot give what they no longer hold.

Goddess of death and fate. Patron of gravesmen and the forgotten.

## Titles

- The Shepherd of Souls
- She Who Remembers
- Keeper of the Forgotten
- The Quiet Lady
- The Last Door

## The Shepherd of Souls

Morvaine shaped the endings of the world, and the paths that lead to them, and in doing so took on their aspect. Every life that ends passes through her keeping, from the oldest king to the smallest fish in the shallows.

Yet she is a shepherd, not a reaper. She does not hunger for death, nor hasten it, nor take any joy in it. She asks only that the balance of souls be kept: that every life which ends is gathered gently, tended and sent on its way, so that the great turning of the world may continue.

## She Who Remembers

When a soul comes to Morvaine, she takes from it the memories of the life it has lived, and who it was. These she keeps. Then she cleanses the soul, blesses it, and gives it to the Pool of Life, from which a new soul can be built whenever one is needed.

So it is that Morvaine remembers everyone. The pauper thrown into the surf unmourned, the sailor lost at sea, the nation drowned so long ago that not even its name survives: all of them are forgotten by the world, and none of them by her. It is why she is the patron of the forgotten, and why, at every funeral across the islands, the mourners close with the same words: **"Morvaine remembers."**

Over the long centuries of [[Aquilla's Wrath]], she watched the tides of souls shift as the world did. Countless souls that once would have been built for children of the land have instead been born to the sea.

## The Shepherd of Chance

Fate, as Morvaine knows it, is not written in the stars. It is a vast and shifting field of what is likely and what is not, and she is its shepherd, guiding the flock of what may be toward what will be. Chance still has its part to play, which is why she and [[Fennara]] can share the world at all, however much he tests her patience.

Yet even her sight has its limits. It is said that the threads of the gods are hidden even from her, and that the death of [[Arkaius]] was the one ending she never saw coming. Many still whisper that she was the one who killed him, for who else could kill a god but death herself? But if the tale is true, she was as blind to it as anyone.

The flood, too, was only a turning of fate to her. She was not moved by it, but neither did she wish for it. She simply gathered each soul as it came, as she always has, and kept the balance.

## The Black Sheep

The other gods find Morvaine unsettling. Death and fate are cold and mysterious things, and she carries more memory than all of them together. Most keep their distance, and Morvaine has never much cared.

She was fond of [[Arkaius]], and she supports his daughter [[Ellehwin]], though that support is more duty than warmth. Only one god has ever truly reached her: [[Lyandris]], who looked past the chill of death and fate and found the person beneath. She loves his music and his heart, and he loves her as a brother loves a sister.

## The Stolen Soul

When [[Arkaius]] died, his soul should have come to Morvaine, as every soul must. It never arrived. [[Urset]] tore it away before it could reach her, and in doing so he did what no god had ever dared: he stole from death itself. It is a grievance Morvaine will never forget, and she forgets nothing.

Nor does she forgive those who follow in his footsteps. The undead are souls kept from her by force, and the necromancy that binds them is magic drawn from [[Urset]]'s hoard. To Morvaine, every walking corpse is one more theft.

### Temples  
Morvaine's temples are small and quiet places, built at the water's edge and kept by gravesmen and undertakers. They are little more than a place to prepare the dead, build their funeral boats and send them out to sea.

Gravesmen were once the diggers of graves. When the land grew too precious to bury the dead, their old trade vanished, but the name did not. Today, a gravesman prepares the bodies of the dead, builds the boats that will carry them, performs the send-off upon the water and records the name of everyone they send. It is solemn work, and gravesmen are treated with a quiet, wary respect.

Every temple keeps a Book of Names, a ledger of every soul it has sent to the sea. The forgotten are written there too, those with no family left to speak for them, so that even in the mortal world, someone remembers.

Morvaine's temples are also places to pray for the lost. When a sailor is lost at sea and there is no body to send, their family comes to the temple to pray, and the gravesmen write the name into the book all the same.

## Worship

Morvaine is the patron of gravesmen, undertakers and all who tend the dead, and of the forgotten, who have no one else.

There is no longer land enough to bury the dead, and so most are given to the sea. Their bodies are laid upon funeral boats, set alight and pushed out onto the waves, and their souls go on to Morvaine. Every funeral ends with the same words: "Morvaine remembers."

Fortune-tellers and seers of every kind claim her blessing, reading the shape of what is likely to come. Most are frauds. Now and then, one is not.

## The Night of Names

Once each year, on the 22nd of Falris, comes the [[Night of Names]], when the islands honor their dead.

The day is spent in preparation. Families build small lantern boats, one for each loved one they have lost, and write the names of the dead upon them. Meanwhile, the leaders of every town build one great lantern boat together. It bears no names at all, for it is made in honor of all the forgotten, and of Morvaine, the shepherd of souls herself.

As night falls, the great lantern boat is set alight and pushed out onto the water, and the small boats follow behind it, a whole harbor of drifting lights led out to sea while funeral songs are sung from the shore. As each boat is launched, the name upon it is spoken aloud, and the crowd answers as one: **"Morvaine remembers."**

When the last boat has been launched, a moment of silence falls over the harbor. Then the mourning ends, and the festival begins. The rest of the night belongs to the living: to those still here, and to those yet to come.

It is said that on the Night of Names, souls can feel some faint hint of who they once were. Fortune-tellers do their best business of the year that night, and many claim it is the one night they can read the life a person lived before. Whether any of them truly can, no one can say.

### Champion

Morvaine's champion is the best-kept secret in all of divinity. No one knows who they are or what they do. Not even a rumor has ever escaped.

## Relationships

- **[[Lyandris]]**: Her dearest friend, and the only god who has ever truly seen her. She loves his music and his heart, and some say she smiles only for him.
- **[[Fennara]]**: Fate and chance. [[Fennara]] adores her, and she can barely stand him, though the two have always managed to share the world.
- **[[Arkaius]]**: She was fond of him, and his death was the one ending she never foresaw. Many still whisper that she was the one who caused it.
- **[[Ellehwin]]**: Morvaine supports her rule, though more out of duty than warmth.
- **[[Urset]]**: He stole a soul from death itself. She will never forget it, and she forgets nothing.
- **[[Apiot]]**: [[Apiot]] accepts her as part of the natural cycle, the end that makes room for new growth, and the two share a quiet understanding.

God of war. Patron of warriors and generals.

## Titles

- The Strategist
- The Iron General
- Master of the Field
- The Unbeaten
- Lord of the Long Game

## The Art of War

Tarrakus shaped the art of war: not its fury or its bloodlust, but its discipline, its order and its cold, unforgiving arithmetic. In doing so, he took on its aspect, and he has been as patient and precise as a well-laid battle plan ever since.

He is a cold god, but never a cruel one. To Tarrakus, war is a tool. Sometimes it is necessary, but it is never to be wasted, and it is always to be won at the smallest possible cost. He despises needless slaughter as much as he despises a poorly made plan, and the two, in his eyes, are very often the same thing. He thinks three moves ahead of everyone around him, and he is very rarely wrong.

Yet no one should mistake his calm for gentleness. Tarrakus seldom takes the field himself, but if he ever chose to, he would be a force of nature, a storm of steel and strategy that no army in the world could hope to stand against.

## A Lower God

Tarrakus sits among the lower gods, for war, however terrible, touches less of the world than the land, the sea or the wild. Yet his place among them suits the upper gods well. Few of them would admit it, but they fear what the god of war might do with a seat among them, and they are content to keep him where he is.

## War Upon the Waves

For most of history, war meant armies marching across open land. Then the waters rose. Through the long centuries of [[Aquilla's Wrath]], the battlefields drowned, and war moved to the sea. Fleets replaced armies, boarding actions replaced cavalry charges, and the roar of cannon replaced the clash of shield walls. Wars raged as the sea folk claimed the drowning lands from those who lived upon them.

Tarrakus adapted faster than anyone. Today, he is as much the patron of admirals, marines and naval gunners as he ever was of generals and foot soldiers.

## The Rules of War

Tarrakus holds that even war must have its laws, and his faithful keep them as sacred:

- **Parley is sacred.** No one may be harmed beneath a flag of truce. It is the most sacred of all the Rules of War.
- **Surrender is honored.** A foe who lays down their arms is to be spared.
- **Prisoners are kept, not slaughtered.** They are fed, sheltered and returned when the fighting ends.
- **The fallen are honored.** The dead of both sides are given to the sea with respect.

To break the Rules of War is among the gravest crimes a soldier can commit, and a commander known to have done so will find that no honorable soldier in the world will serve beneath them.

Pirates have a tenuous relationship with the god of war. Some crews keep his Rules as faithfully as any navy, while others pay them no mind at all, and a sailor can never be sure which kind of pirate they will meet. Yet even the most ruthless pirates respect the parley. A flag of truce is honored on every sea, by every crew, for to break the parley is to turn every ship in the world against you.

## The War Games

Tarrakus is famous for his games of strategy, and it is said that the gods themselves sometimes sit across the board from him. He has never once lost.

In his honor, mortals across the islands play a game of strategy called Tarrak, played upon a board of squares with carved pieces for ships and soldiers. It is a fixture of every barracks, every warship and most taverns, and the finest Tarrak players are said to make the finest commanders. To swear that one will beat Tarrakus at his own game is one of the oldest and most beloved boasts at [[Hearthfire's Blaze]].

## Worship

Tarrakus is the patron of warriors and generals, of admirals and marines, of mercenaries and of every soldier who has ever held a line.

- **The silent salute.** Before battle, soldiers salute Tarrakus in silence.
- **Lessons in scars.** Old soldiers say that every scar is a lesson Tarrakus taught them, and few of them would trade a single one.
- **The map table.** His shrines stand in war rooms, fortresses and on the decks of flagships, and their altars are map tables, where commanders lay out their plans before him.

In military cities, and in the ports of call where mercenaries gather, stand the temples of Tarrakus. They are as much war colleges as places of worship. Their priests are often retired admirals, generals and strategists, who spend their final years teaching what a lifetime of war has taught them. Alongside them serve the new blood: young men and women learning strategy before setting out on their new lives as soldiers.

For anyone who would lead others into battle, a season in Tarrakus's church is a rite of passage. Would-be commanders of every kind, from ship's captains to generals, are inducted into the church and study there until the high priest deems them ready. Few will follow a commander who has not.

### Champion

No one knows whether Tarrakus has a champion. If he does, they are surely hidden, as everything about the god of war is hidden until the moment it matters. Yet there are rumors. Many of the greatest strategists in history, generals and admirals whose victories still seem impossible, are whispered to have been his champions. No one has ever proven it.

## Relationships

- **[[Urset]]**: The closest thing [[Urset]] has to an ally, and a puzzling choice of friend for a god as level-headed and calculating as Tarrakus. No one understands it.
- **[[Ellehwin]]**: The law and the Rules of War are not so different, and [[Ellehwin]] has always found Tarrakus to be one of the most dependable gods in the pantheon.
- **[[Flaris]]**: Guest-right binds even enemies, and Tarrakus honors it as faithfully as any of [[Flaris|Flaris's]] own followers.
- **[[Fennara]]**: A plan that relies on luck is a bad plan, and Tarrakus has no patience for the god of fortune. [[Fennara]] finds this endlessly entertaining, and never stops reminding him that even the best-laid plans need a little luck.
- **[[Koruk]]**: In the old days, Koruk's dragons were the greatest threat any army could face, and Tarrakus respected their power while despising their mindless fury. He finds the calmed Koruk a far more interesting opponent across the game board.
- **[[Arkaius]]**: The most cynical whisper that Tarrakus had a hand in the death of [[Arkaius]]. He has never dignified the rumor with an answer.
